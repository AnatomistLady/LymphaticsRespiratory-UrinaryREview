1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab, you will be able to:

  • Identify the histological architecture of lymphoid organs (lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, and MALT) using OpenStax 2e reference micrographs.
  • Differentiate the microscopic structures and functional compartments of the spleen (red pulp vs. white pulp) and lymph nodes (cortex vs. medulla).
  • Locate and name regional lymph node clusters, tonsils, and visceral lymphatic organs on anatomical models.
  • Map the systemic drainage territories of the Right Lymphatic Duct versus the Thoracic Duct into the venous circulation.

2. Systematic Lymphatic Anatomy & Histology Directory

Lymphoid Histology, Organs, and Drainage Territories (Lab Manual Exercise 5)
Organ / Tissue Diagnostic Microscopic Structures (OpenStax A&P 2e) Key Functional / Gross Landmarks
Lymph Node
  • Fibrous capsule & Trabeculae
  • Subcapsular sinus & Trabecular sinus
  • Outer cortex with germinal centers
  • Inner cortex (paracortex)
  • Medulla (Medullary cords & Medullary sinuses)
 Filters lymph; resident macrophages and clustered T and B lymphocytes purge pathogens. Located along collecting vessels.
Spleen
  • Fibrous capsule & Trabeculae
  • White Pulp: Germinal centers surrounding central arterioles
  • Red Pulp: Splenic cords (cords of Billroth) and venous sinusoids filled with RBCs
 Filters blood; red pulp removes senescent erythrocytes; white pulp mounts adaptive immune responses. Features splenic vessels and hilum.
Thymus Gland
  • Fibrous capsule & Connective tissue septa
  • Thymic lobules
  • Cortex (densely packed thymocytes)
  • Medulla (paler core containing epithelial reticular cells)
 Bilobed primary organ in superior mediastinum between sternum and aorta; site of T-lymphocyte maturation and immunocompetence.
Lymphoid Nodules & Tonsils
  • Unencapsulated dense lymphocyte clusters
  • Germinal centers
  • Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue (MALT)
  • Peyer's patches (aggregated nodules in ileum)
 Frontline defense intercepting ingested/inhaled environmental pathogens:
  • Pharyngeal tonsil (adenoid)
  • Palatine tonsils
  • Lingual tonsil (base of tongue)
Regional Lymph Nodes Macroscopic encapsulated node groupings located throughout the body:
  • Cervical lymph nodes (neck)
  • Axillary lymph nodes (armpit/chest)
  • Inguinal lymph nodes (groin)
 Filter lymph draining from head/neck, upper limbs/mammary tissue, and lower extremities/pelvis respectively.
Lymphatic Ducts & Trunks
  • Cisterna Chyli: Dilated sac anterior to lumbar vertebrae
  • Thoracic Duct: Ascends through mediastinum
  • Right Lymphatic Duct: Short terminal duct on right side
 Venous Terminations:
• Right Lymphatic Duct: Drains right upper quadrant; empties into junction of Right Internal Jugular & Right Subclavian Veins.
• Thoracic Duct: Drains entire lower body, left thorax, left upper limb, left head/neck; empties into junction of Left Internal Jugular & Left Subclavian Veins.

Activity 3: Clinical & Applied Pathology

  1. Lymphedema: Lymphatic vessels function as an essential one-way drainage system that retrieves excess interstitial fluid not reabsorbed by blood capillaries. Surgical removal of axillary lymph nodes and collecting vessels severs the drainage pathway for the arm, causing protein-rich fluid to accumulate in the interstitial spaces of the limb.
  2. Splenic Rupture: The spleen receives substantial arterial blood flow directly from the celiac trunk via the large splenic artery and lacks a heavy protective fibrous capsule. Lacerations tear the delicate red pulp sinusoids and splenic cords, resulting in massive intraperitoneal hemorrhage and rapid hypovolemic shock.
